## Childhood/Growing Up

I guess this is my introduction to the world wide web! I've never really considered myself as someone with a story to tell but figured I'd give it my best shot.
My name is Adam Trommlitz and I was born and raised in Clinton, UT. I have a wonderful family and I'm the 3rd of 4 kids which yes makes me the middle child and I wholeheartedly relate to being the somewhat forgotten middle child.

Growing up, I feel like I had a great childhood. My parents created a wonderful home where I was raised on good Christian values. I have always loved trying new things and playing games. I got my first Gameboy at age 8 and loved playing a Harry Potter game solving puzzles and figuring out how to beat the game. As I got older I played similar games in Zelda and Kingdom Hearts. I've truly loved problem solving and puzzles and exploring.
I also grew up playing competitive soccer and have loved being active while competing to win. In High School, I took up volleyball and was a setter on our team for 4 years and a captain for our team. These traits of trying new things, figuring out puzzles, and a competitive nature have helped me in my pursuit of solving problems and making a difference in places that I work.

## Mission

After graduating high school, I served an LDS Mission in São Paulo, Brazil (1 year) and Provo, UT (10 months). Teaching my religion to others helped teach me rejection, working through objections, and working hard for long hours. It taught me more empathy for others and listening skills to truly understand them.
I loved being able to serve others and understand a culture that was far different than my own. My experience taught me much that I will take with me the rest of my life.

## College

While on my mission, I decided to attend Brigham Young University and enrolled in classes shortly after I returned back from my mission.
When I arrived as a freshman, I knew I wanted to do something in business but wasn't sure exactly what I wanted to do in business, so I enrolled in some generals and a marketing class.
I had thought about doing finance as my first choice because I liked the idea of learning how to grow mine and others money while getting paid to do it. Seemed like a great way to grow my money in the fastest way.

During the winter semester of my freshman year, I took the intro to accounting and finance classes and I actually really loved what I was learning and felt that I was somewhat good at it. However, I wasn't sure that I actually loved it and could see myself doing it as a career.
During my finance class, our professor, Jim Brau, sent out a sign-up sheet to go biking with him at the gym and have a 30-minute chat. During this conversation, he talked about other majors and counseled that if I wasn't absolutely loving finance then it might not be the right path for me. He mentioned that Information Systems was a growing major with real big opportunities in the tech industry.
I had never really considered information systems because I didn't want to learn computer languages and become a programmer, but he taught me that there was much more to the major than just coding and programming languages. As I thought about his suggestion, I reached out to my brother-in-law who had completed the Master's of Information Systems Management program a few years prior to get his perspective.
When talking with him, I learned even more about the major and all the opportunities one could pursue. He told me that if I wasn't sure what I wanted to do in IS that eventually I would find something that would fit.
So I took him up on his offer and started taking the first information systems classes which I absolutely loved. We learned about a variety of things including databases, SQL, Tableau, Excel and VBA, HTML and CSS, and a variety of other technical terms.

After taking the other intro to information systems classes, I applied for the program and was accepted! From there I learned even more about SQL, Python, Javascript, Product Management, Cybersecurity, Statistics, C#, and so much more. It was a blast! I loved my professors and the students with whom I was learning this with.
While the classes were fascinating, I wasn't sure where exactly I wanted to learn more and possibly do an internship in. Around this time, the tech market took a big hit with companies scaling back after over-hiring during the pandemic and the tech craze that followed.
With the market looking uncertain and not having any technical experience outside the classroom, I began to apply for as many internships as possible. That is when I applied for an internship as a data analyst at Intermountain Health.
After a single round of interviews, I was offered the position which I quickly accepted.

## Internships

During my internship, I developed a ton of skills with SQL and Tableau. I had an amazing mentor who took me under her wing to teach me a lot of the principles for being successful in the role. She also gave me plenty of time and encouragement to try new things and take my time on difficult problems.
In my role, I got to work with the Nurse and Medical Assistant Residency teams who are responsible for all the nurses and medical assistants in the organization who are hired and need to take the required courses to complete their residency. I developed over 10+ dashboards to help make tracking their progress through residency much faster and without using Excel.
It was extremely rewarding to be able to use my skills to make a meaningful difference for their team. Additionally, I developed dashboards and reporting tools for the Simulation teams who are responsible for running simulations to upskill nurses and doctors as well as provide new locations with the adequate training they need to get started.
At the conclusion of the summer, I was extended through the next 8 months of school.

During this same summer, I applied for BYU's Master of Information Systems Management program and was accepted. This integrated program allows one to get their bachelors and masters degree at the same time in 5 years. During this time, I wanted to go more down the data route because I loved my internship and the powers of data.
I took more classes in data science and data engineering. When applying for different internships, I came across Lucid Software at a career fair. I initially wanted to apply for their analytics engineering internship; however, after applying they recommended that I apply for the product management role.
I knew a little bit about product management but figured it was worth interviewing for. After going through a recruiter call, a technical interview, and an interview with one of the Sr. Directors, I was offered a position. I wasn't sold on taking it because I wanted to work more in data.
However, after talking with a friend who was a PM intern at the company a year prior and learning about how much data is involved at a software company, I decided to take the role and I haven't looked back since.

Now I'm currently working at Lucid Software for the summer learning how to be a better product manager!
